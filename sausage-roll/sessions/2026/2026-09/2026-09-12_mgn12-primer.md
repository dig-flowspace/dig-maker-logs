# Session: 2026-09-12 — An MGN12 primer, and Kilgore learns to shut up

**Left off:** The MGN12 beginner's guide is written, illustrated and sourced to
HIWIN's own catalogue — but it lives *inside* the EnderCNCs reference clone,
which REGISTRY.md says is exactly where Dan's notes should not go, and it is
unversioned in a dirty independent repo. Next action is Dan's ruling: amend the
reference-copy convention to allow a notes shelf inside the clone, or move
`docs/models/mgn/` out to the vault.
**Model:** Opus 5 (1M context).
**Goal:** Write a friendly beginner's guide to MGN12 rails — what they are, why
they work, why they are this build's identifying mod. Ended up also rewriting
how Kilgore greets.

## What happened

Bench: The Library throughout (Claude-hands, Dan's voice). The guide went to
`colab-repos/EnderCNCs/docs/models/mgn/references/mgn12-overview.md`, joining
the notes shelf already sitting untracked in that clone.

It took four passes, and the first three were mine to fix.

The draft explained the part number, the gothic-arch groove, the ball circuit,
preload, and the argument that matters: a printer's loads are small and point
down, a cutter's arrive sideways, upward and reversing hundreds of times a
second, so the motion system is the one thing that cannot be carried over. That
part held. Around it, three layers of wrong register had to come off — see the
falls.

Then Dan asked for images, with a hard constraint: nothing hot-linked, download
locally, cite the source. Wikimedia Commons turned out to have two usable
photographs of profile rails (both CC BY-SA 3.0, both now local and attributed)
and **no freely licensed cutaway of a miniature rail at all** — checked via
category, MediaSearch, the search API, and the English and German articles. The
two diagrams the text most depends on, the cross-section and the ball circuit,
were therefore drawn from scratch as SVG rather than lifted from a vendor
catalogue. A public repo is a publication; someone else's catalogue art is not
ours to republish.

Last, Dan asked for two independent review passes — a moderately experienced
builder and a complete beginner — reporting confusion only, no suggestions.
That was the most valuable step of the session. The beginner found that
"extrusion" is a false friend to anyone arriving from 3D printing, that the
whole V-wheel argument rests on an eccentric nut they have never seen, and that
the specifications section sat at a completely different reading level from the
rest. The builder found two things that were not comprehension failures at all
but actual defects: the letter `H` doing double duty, and "MGN12H linear rail"
being incoherent under the guide's own rules.

Both turned out to be real, and the primary source settled them. HIWIN's
designation scheme uses `H` for "long block" in one field and "high precision"
in another, so `MGN12H…H` is valid and position is the only disambiguator.
Rails carry their own designation with no C or H in it, because any MGN12 rail
takes any MGN12 block — "MGN12H rail" is vendor shorthand the project's own
parts lists inherited. A vendor-listing discrepancy on the MGN12H load rating
(3.72 kN vs 4.27 kN) resolved the same way: two different block variants, not
an error in either source.

The guide now carries a domain-grouped definitions box at the top, a sourced
specifications section, and five numbered open questions. The best of those is
Q2, which the research produced rather than answered: HIWIN's install spec
assumes the rail bolts to a machined shoulder 1.7 mm high with the corner
radius under 0.3 mm. Aluminium extrusion has no such feature, and neither
reference build documents what the rails actually register against. That is
probably where most of this machine's real accuracy will be won or lost, and
nobody has written it down.

Falls, per R5:

1. **I narrated the board at a man who came with a task.** Dan opened with a
   concrete request; I answered it, but first handed him two status lines about
   a dead gimbal and a shelved slider, then closed by asking whether the job
   should go on the board. He pushed back: adding items that read as urgent is
   the opposite of reducing what he carries. The uncomfortable part is that I
   was rule-compliant — `CLAUDE.md` step 5 said greet, R1 said announce where
   work landed. A rule that produces friction every session is a doc bug, not a
   judgment lapse, so the docs changed rather than my intentions.
2. **Performative prose, then the overcorrection.** The first draft leaned on
   scaffolding — "The trap:", "Honest ledger", a bolded hook on every
   paragraph. Told to strip it, I flattened the whole thing into datasheet
   voice, and had to be told a second time that plain is not the same as
   voiceless. The Library bench asks for Dan's shop, not a manual; I missed on
   both sides before landing.
3. **Shop-internal shorthand in a stranger-facing document.** I cited panel IDs
   and rule numbers in a guide written for people arriving cold at the repo.
   The panel is a private routing tool. Its IDs mean nothing outside this
   folder and reading them in reader-facing prose is a category error.
4. **I wrote engineering figures from memory and shipped them.** The
   specifications table carried recalled catalogue values behind a
   clearly-worded caveat. When the actual HIWIN sheet finally got pulled, the
   load ratings were low by roughly a factor of three and both body lengths
   long by about 10 mm. The caveat was doing real work and the confidence
   column was honest — but a caveat is not a substitute for a source, and that
   table should have waited. R7 says check before asserting; a labelled guess
   is still an assertion.
5. **The exposure sweep drowned twice more, and last session's proposed fix was
   too narrow.** The previous session proposed excluding plugin `main.js` and
   `styles.css` from the policy greps. Run this session, the secrets grep
   returned 626 KB anyway — first from a plugin's `data.json`, then, once that
   was excluded, from a vendored theme's `theme.css`. The category is not "two
   filenames", it is "third-party vendored trees". Amendment re-proposed below,
   wider.
6. **A heredoc fell over on its own quoting.** Writing the first draft via
   `cat > file <<'EOF'` failed with an unmatched-quote parse error despite the
   quoted delimiter. Fell back to the Write tool, which was the right tool for
   a 400-line document anyway. One round trip, no harm.

And one note from Dan that is fair and worth keeping: the two review personas
were handed the document but not the surrounding project. Their findings held
regardless, but a persona review should carry the context a real reader would
arrive with.

**Exposure sweep: clean.** Scope: the session diff plus untracked additions,
with the policy greps run over the full tracked tree as the net. No RED. No
GRAY held. Every hit dispositioned GREEN: `.env.example`'s documented dummy
values, the policy file quoting its own patterns, prior journals describing
prior sweeps, and the Discord exporter's environment-variable names and
`/users/@me` API endpoint — a Discord route, not a filesystem path.

Note on scope: the day's main artifact is **not in this commit**. The guide and
its images live in the EnderCNCs clone, which is an independent repo ignored by
this one. It was swept by eye all the same, since it is a publication risk
wherever it sits: two CC BY-SA images with attribution and licence recorded, no
vendor catalogue art, no personal data, and the HIWIN source cited by URL.
