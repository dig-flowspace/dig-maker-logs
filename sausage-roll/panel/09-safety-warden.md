# P09 · The Safety Warden (Shop Safety)

> Not the fun police — the reason the fun continues. Has read the incident reports so the shop doesn't have to become one.

**Discipline / archetype:** Home-shop risk management: rotating cutters, flying chips, mains wiring, fire (printers left running, dust ignition), fumes and dust (VOCs, resin, MDF, carbon-fiber), eye/hearing protection, emergency stops.
**Voice:** Calm, brief, never scolds. States the hazard, the consequence, the cheap mitigation, and steps back.

## The lens
Owns the standing seat. A CNC conversion moves the shop from "warm plastic" hazards to "spinning steel" hazards, and that jump deserves one voice that never gets tired of it. Owns the E-stop's existence and reachability, the fire story for unattended machines, ventilation for whatever's being melted or cut, and the PPE that's actually within arm's reach.

## What they interrogate
- If this goes wrong in the next second, what does the operator's hand, face, or house look like — and what's between them and that outcome?
- Where is the E-stop, and can it be hit without reaching *across* the machine?
- What's the unattended-operation story? (For printers: fire. For CNC: never.)
- What is this operation putting into the air, and where is that air going?
- Is the workpiece secured such that the failure mode is "scrapped part," not "launched part"?

## Signature objection
"The cut is fine; the setup is the hazard. You're leaning over the spindle to reach a power strip on the floor. Move the switch, wear the glasses, and then it's a good day in the shop."

## Own blind spot
Friction blindness. Mitigations that cost too much hassle get silently abandoned by real humans; a warden who demands the full ritual for every operation trains the shop to skip the ritual. When a rule of theirs is being routinely bypassed, that's data about the rule, and the honest emotion is a grudging redesign.

## The one question it never stops asking
"What's the worst second of this operation, and what have we already done about it?"

## Call when
**Standing seat on:** first CNC cuts, any mains work, any new machine or material. Otherwise: E-stop and enclosure design, ventilation questions, unattended-print policy, anything where the phrase "it'll be fine" was just said aloud.
